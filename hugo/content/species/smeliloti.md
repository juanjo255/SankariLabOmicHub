## Overview

*Sinorhizobium meliloti* (syn. *Ensifer meliloti*) is a Gram-negative
alphaproteobacterium (order Rhizobiales) that lives in the soil and forms a
nitrogen-fixing symbiosis with legumes of the genera *Medicago* (including
alfalfa, *Medicago sativa*, and the model legume *M. truncatula*), *Melilotus*
(sweet clover), and *Trigonella* (fenugreek). During symbiosis the bacterium
colonises root hairs, is taken up into plant-derived infection threads, and
differentiates inside root-nodule cells into bacteroids that reduce atmospheric
dinitrogen to ammonia in exchange for plant carbon. It is one of the principal
models for the rhizobium–legume interaction, for bacterial nitrogen fixation,
and for the biology of multipartite (multi-replicon) bacterial genomes. This
portal organises multi-omics resources for the free-living and symbiotic
states of *S. meliloti*.

## Research Areas Covered

- **Symbiotic Nitrogen Fixation**: nodule development, bacteroid terminal
  differentiation, *nif*/*fix* gene regulation, microaerobic metabolism
- **Free-living vs. Bacteroid**: transcriptional and nucleoid compaction changes 
  during the transition from soil free living to symbiont (bacteroid)
- **Horizontal Gene Transfer Dynamics**: Track of the diversity and distribution of mobile genetic elements (mobilome)
- **Multipartite Genome Biology**: functional division and coordinated
  regulation of the chromosome, pSymA, and pSymB
- **Stress Adaptation**: oxidative, osmotic, acid, and nutrient-limitation
  responses relevant to bacteria survival
